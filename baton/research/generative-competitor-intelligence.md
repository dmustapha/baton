# Generative Competitor Intelligence: Flare Summer Signal

**Purpose:** Convert the complete 99-signal public competitor corpus into creative raw material for Warroom. This is an opportunity map seed, not an idea shortlist and not a kill list.

## Evidence boundary

This synthesis derives from all 87 likely-current repository projects, four profile-recovered repositories, and eight profile-only signals in [`roster-intelligence.md`](roster-intelligence.md). The machine-readable source is [`roster-intelligence.json`](roster-audit/derived/roster-intelligence.json). Public evidence establishes disclosed mechanisms and proof quality, not formal submission, private work, team composition, or prize eligibility. [C2]

## What the field has already proved

| Proven pattern | Evidence across the corpus | Generative value |
|---|---|---|
| External-chain facts can trigger useful Flare outcomes | FDC payment, non-payment, Web2Json, treasury, inheritance, insurance, and reconciliation flows | Start from a consequential external event, then ask which next outcome remains manual or unsafe |
| Confidential computation is most legible when it releases a minimal signed result | Credit eligibility, policy signing, private routing, solvency, payroll, and document claims | Design the private boundary around one necessary decision, not generalized “privacy” |
| Judges can understand complex infrastructure through receipts | Strong projects show exact transactions, verified contracts, tests, videos, status states, and limitation matrices | Make evidence production part of the product experience |
| Negative evidence creates powerful automation | Backstop and Heirloom use nonexistence or inactivity to trigger insurance or inheritance behavior | Search for other lifecycle absences whose safe resolution is still manual |
| Policy-controlled signing can remove operator custody | Keyless and PMW-shaped systems separate authorization policy from raw key possession | Treat confidential authorization and recovery as a programmable product primitive |
| Honest trust-boundary disclosure increases credibility | Several projects distinguish mock assets, simulated enclaves, registered extensions, and roadmap claims | Expose simulation, attestation, registration, and production states explicitly |
| Exception handling is product value | Insurance, risk, challenger, exit-liquidity, and verification projects exist because happy-path asset movement is insufficient | Generate around what happens after minting, signing, proving, or settlement fails |

## What remains incomplete

These are evidence-backed opportunity questions, not claims that no private solution exists.

| Missing or weakly served outcome | Corpus evidence | Question Warroom must explore |
|---|---|---|
| End-to-end FCC operational assurance | Many projects consume FCC-shaped services; few own registration, machine rotation, indexer health, attestation checks, and result verification together | What user outcome becomes possible when confidential execution is observable and recoverable? |
| Safe recovery for policy-controlled external signing | Keyless and BridgeSafe demonstrate policy signing; rotation, liveness failure, disaster recovery, and pre-execution simulation remain fragmented | Which asset action becomes materially safer when recovery is part of the authorization lifecycle? |
| Active resolution of stuck FAssets states | Solvency, redemption depth, challengers, insurance, and monitoring are crowded; diagnosis often stops before safe recovery | Which exception can the product actually resolve rather than merely display? |
| Joined confidential plus interoperable outcome | Many dual-lane projects place privacy and asset features beside each other; fewer prove that the private decision is necessary for the external-asset action | Which asset transition must depend on a verifiable confidential operation? |
| Reusable multi-primitive failure evidence | Builders repeatedly reconstruct FDC waits, FCC boundaries, Coston2 fixtures, RPC failure handling, and explorer proof | How can failure injection produce an outcome a user or operator values, not only developer telemetry? |
| First-user distribution for infrastructure | Strong repositories prove mechanisms but often lack a sharp adoption wedge | Which existing Flare actor encounters the failure today and can use the product without a marketplace? |

## Portable mechanism inventory

Warroom may recombine these mechanisms without copying the source products:

1. FDC-confirmed presence or absence as a state-transition trigger.
2. A confidential computation that emits only a signed minimum-necessary result.
3. Policy-controlled external-chain signing without operator key custody.
4. Stake or collateral backing a privately computed public claim.
5. Explicit liveness, rotation, recovery, and abstention states.
6. Append-only evidence receipts that connect offchain work to onchain settlement.
7. Pre-execution simulation followed by policy authorization and exact transaction proof.
8. Contract Registry resolution instead of hardcoded protocol assumptions.
9. Failure-path staging as the central demo mechanic.
10. Honest capability matrices separating live, simulated, attested, registered, and planned behavior.

## Multi-track architecture space

Flare Summer Signal uses the `multi-track required` strategy for Warroom. Every concept must combine one real interoperable asset lifecycle with one necessary confidential operation.

| Interoperable asset side | Confidential side | Joined question |
|---|---|---|
| FAssets mint, redeem, liquidation, or recovery | FCC policy, eligibility, risk, or exception decision | Why must this confidential result authorize or change the asset transition? |
| XRPL or other external-chain payment attested by FDC | Confidential recipient, policy, claim, or intent evaluation | What minimum result can be revealed while keeping sensitive inputs private? |
| Smart Account authorization | FCC or PMW policy execution and signer control | Which action is impossible safely without both XRPL-linked authority and confidential policy enforcement? |
| PMW external-chain transaction | Confidential policy simulation, signing, rotation, or recovery | What judge-visible receipt proves the TEE-controlled decision caused the external asset action? |
| FXRP utility or settlement | Confidential computation whose output changes terms or execution | Does removing privacy change the economic outcome, or merely remove a feature? |

## Mandatory removal tests

Every finalist must answer both:

1. **Remove Interoperable Assets:** If FAssets, FXRP, Smart Accounts, FDC-verified asset state, or PMW external execution disappears, what necessary economic action can no longer occur?
2. **Remove Confidential Compute:** If FCC, FCE, or the verifiable TEE operation disappears, what necessary privacy, policy, authorization, or execution guarantee fails?

If either answer is “the same product still works with less branding,” reject the concept.

## Joined proof-path requirement

Every finalist must demonstrate one continuous path:

```text
named user and costly trigger
  → real external or FAsset state
  → necessary confidential computation or authorization
  → verified onchain or external-chain asset action
  → exact receipt, transaction, attestation status, and failure safeguard
```

Two unrelated feature tabs are not dual-track depth. The confidential operation must causally control, authorize, price, recover, or verify the interoperable asset outcome.

## Generator contract

Every Warroom generator receives this same corpus-derived map and must produce, for each idea:

- one demonstrated strength or portable mechanism;
- one missing outcome, underserved job, or unserved edge state;
- a named user with a costly current failure;
- a distinct load-bearing primitive for each track;
- both removal-test answers;
- one joined proof path;
- only then, after the raw concept is recorded, a collision analysis against the complete registry.

The corpus is generative evidence first and defensive evidence second.
