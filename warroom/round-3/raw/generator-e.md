# Generator E — Round 3

## 1. SendSure

- **Name:** SendSure
- **Problem:** A person can send money to the wrong destination, send less than a required minimum, or approve a route that no longer works. A warning is easy to click through; after the send, the mistake may be irreversible.
- **Market Anchor:** Self-custodied XRP holders already enter FXRP through direct mint or Smart Accounts and deploy it into existing strategies. Current FXRP supply, millions of FXRP DeFi transactions, high deployment of circulating FXRP, and funded wallet-deposit campaigns show an active first-use funnel where a bad send has immediate cost.
- **Named Buyer:** A self-custodied XRP holder using an existing XRP wallet and holder-owned Flare account.
- **Existing Workflow:** Enter an amount and destination, sign an XRP instruction, wait for proof, receive FXRP, then deploy it into an existing application or vault.
- **Current Substitute:** Wallet warnings, manual address checks, manual preflight, support documentation, and native recovery controls. These explain risk but do not turn the holder's private purpose and limits into an enforceable condition on the eventual asset action.
- **Mechanism:** Before signing, the holder privately supplies the intended amount range, destination class, minimum acceptable amount, expiry, and target-strategy preference. FCC/FCE checks the exact proposed action and returns a signed `SAFE_TO_SEND`, `CORRECTABLE`, or `STOP` result, revealing only a reason code, action hash, bound, and expiry. A verifier contract accepts only a result tied to the current Smart Account instruction. `CORRECTABLE` offers one bounded replacement action for the holder to sign; it never silently rewrites the transfer.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** The safety decision gates an XRPL-authorized Smart Account action that enters or uses the XRP-to-FXRP lifecycle. FDC supplies the external payment proof and the final receipt connects the XRP instruction to the resulting FXRP state. Without this cross-chain authorization and asset consequence, SendSure is only a generic form validator.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through a real XRP instruction, FDC evidence, FXRP state change, and Smart Account execution; Confidential Compute Apps through necessary private policy evaluation, minimum disclosure, declared machine/attestation status, signed output, and contract verification. Contract Registry resolves mutable protocol addresses.
- **Demo Hook:** Enter a destination that conflicts with the holder's private rule and an amount below the required minimum. The Send button becomes a plain-language “Stopped before money moved” receipt. Accept the bounded correction, sign it, and show the XRP proof, FXRP arrival, and exact Flare transaction in one timeline.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The asset-entry cluster is good at guiding people through a complicated happy path, but weak when a wrong reference, stale route, or amount mismatch appears. The opportunity is a guardrail that changes the allowed action, not another warning screen.
- **Missing Outcome:** The holder gets either a safely completed first use or a provable stop before value moves, without publishing why the destination or amount is sensitive.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: holder signs XRP instruction → FDC proof → FXRP mint/use state → Smart Account action → receipt. Confidential track: private purpose and limits → FCC/FCE pre-action check → signed bounded result → verifier. Execution requires both the holder's authorization and a current safe result for the identical action hash.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: no XRP-to-FXRP transition or Smart Account action remains, so the product is an ordinary payment checklist. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: the holder must expose sensitive purpose, recipient rules, and limits or settle for a non-enforceable warning; the private guardrail fails.
- **Proof Path:** Holder enters one unsafe action → private inputs are committed → FCC/FCE runs with visible status label → verifier rejects the action and emits the stop receipt → holder signs the offered bounded correction → FDC proves the XRP payment → Smart Account completes the FXRP action → before/after balances and transaction references show success. A repeated stale result is rejected by expiry.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** Holder supplies private rules and alone signs the XRP instruction → FCC/FCE evaluates but cannot move funds → verifier checks result signature, action hash, bound, and expiry → FDC attests only the external payment → holder's controller/Personal Account calls the existing application or vault → receipt. The app neither takes custody nor controls native FAssets assignment or capacity.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts the pre-action/post-action guardian and verifiable private decision to a phrase any wallet user understands: “stop me before I send the wrong amount.” First-five-user reach comes through five existing XRP wallet users, with no marketplace or new counterparty required. If FCC/FCE is not live, the demo labels the leg `SIMULATED`, `REGISTERED`, or `ATTESTED` exactly as evidenced rather than claiming production secrecy.

## 2. Redemption Finish Line

- **Name:** Redemption Finish Line
- **Problem:** A customer is waiting to get their money back, but the operator's first payment was incomplete or its proof is late. The operations team must decide quickly whether to pay the remainder, yet a rushed second payment can overpay or create a larger treasury problem.
- **Market Anchor:** Registered FAssets agents already fulfill assigned redemptions, earn mint/redemption fee shares, maintain locked collateral, and face liquidation, default, and challenge exposure. Repeated historical agent activity supplies reachable operators and real exception cases.
- **Named Buyer:** A registered FAssets agent's treasury or operations lead.
- **Existing Workflow:** Receive a native redemption assignment, arrange the XRP payment, prove it, monitor the proof window and collateral state, and resolve exceptions using the agent console and treasury process.
- **Current Substitute:** Agent console, internal treasury tools, public monitoring, and native default, challenge, liquidation, and proof mechanisms. These show what happened or impose consequences; they do not privately determine and authorize the exact safe completion amount.
- **Mechanism:** FDC establishes the payment amount present or absent for an existing assignment. FCC/FCE compares that evidence with the agent's private liquidity position, exposure ceiling, emergency policy, and planned movements. It emits a signed `PAY_EXACT_REMAINDER`, `WAIT_FOR_PROOF`, or `ESCALATE` result with assignment hash, amount if any, reason code, and expiry. After verifier approval, only the agent's own wallet may make the corrective XRP payment; a second FDC proof confirms completion.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** The product acts on a native FAssets redemption assignment and closes it with external XRP payment evidence and FDC proof. The app cannot create demand, alter capacity, or substitute another asset; its value is completing a specific XRP-backed redemption safely.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through the assigned FXRP redemption and FDC-confirmed XRP payment; Confidential Compute Apps through private treasury policy, signed exact-action result, minimal disclosure, machine/attestation status, and verifier contract. FAssets addresses come from Contract Registry.
- **Demo Hook:** Show a customer-facing progress bar stuck at “partly paid.” The operator clicks “Finish safely”; the system reveals only the exact remainder or a plain-language wait state. One case completes and turns the bar green after FDC proof; another refuses a duplicate payment while proof is still delayed.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The FAssets risk cluster has many ways to diagnose, score, insure, or punish failure, but fewer ways to complete a bounded recovery. The broad-user improvement is to translate protocol status into “pay this exact remainder,” “wait,” or “escalate.”
- **Missing Outcome:** The waiting customer receives the exact remaining value, or the operator gets a defensible no-payment state, without exposing internal treasury limits.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: redemption assignment → FDC payment evidence → agent-authorized XRP correction → FDC completion proof → updated FAssets state. Confidential track: private liquidity and policy → FCC/FCE decision → signed bounded result → verification. The native correction cannot proceed through the product without the confidential result.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: the customer redemption, XRP payment, proof window, and FAssets completion disappear, leaving a generic operations approval. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: sensitive limits must become public or the exact correction loses its safety basis; the automated finish outcome fails.
- **Proof Path:** Load an existing or honestly labeled deterministic redemption case → display assignment and FDC evidence freshness → run FCC/FCE with declared status → verify the signed result → agent-owned wallet pays only the approved XRP remainder → obtain the second FDC proof → show the completed customer receipt. Replay the same authorization to prove duplicate rejection.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** FAssets protocol supplies the existing assignment → agent treasury owns private policy and payment keys → FDC attests payment facts only → FCC/FCE returns a bounded decision without transaction authority → verifier checks assignment, amount, policy version, and expiry → agent-owned wallet authorizes payment → published FAssets interface consumes proof → receipt. No app-created assignment, liquidity, or operator power is assumed.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts confidential counterfactual simulation and closed-loop recovery to the most understandable agent promise: finish paying the customer without paying twice. The first five users are reachable registered agent operations teams, each able to test against its own historical or current exception workflow.

## 3. FairPartial

- **Name:** FairPartial
- **Problem:** A business and contractor agree that some work was delivered, but disagree about how much. Paying everything feels unsafe; paying nothing is unfair; emailing private files and contract terms to more reviewers makes the dispute worse.
- **Market Anchor:** XRP-funded businesses already manage invoices, counterparties, approvals, private limits, and settlement. The existing payer-payee relationship supplies distribution, while disputed partial work and policy changes are documented weak points in payment and escrow flows. Admission requires an evidenced XRP-funded pilot; no new marketplace is assumed.
- **Named Buyer:** A crypto-native SME treasury or finance lead already controlling XRP, paired with one of its existing contractors.
- **Existing Workflow:** The business receives or holds XRP, reviews private invoice and work evidence, obtains approval, converts or deploys assets, and settles the contractor payment.
- **Current Substitute:** Email review, private accounting/payment software, manual approval, multisig or custody controls, allowlists, and full-pay-or-no-pay negotiation. These can hold funds or hide documents, but they do not make a confidential partial-entitlement result the enforceable release condition.
- **Mechanism:** The business places a bounded amount of its FXRP into a holder-authorized escrow for one existing contractor. The parties commit the agreed milestone rubric and submit private work evidence. FCC/FCE evaluates only that committed rubric and emits a signed `RELEASE(amount)`, `DENY(reasonCode)`, or `REVIEW` result, disclosing no artifact or invoice terms. A verifier contract releases at most the pre-funded amount; an expired, changed-policy, ambiguous, or unavailable-machine result sends the case to review rather than paying.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** XRP-controlled business funds become FXRP, enter a Flare escrow, and move to the contractor only after a contract-verified confidential entitlement decision. Removing FXRP custody and onchain release removes the enforceable partial-settlement outcome.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through a real XRP/FXRP funding and release lifecycle, optionally entered through Smart Account authorization; Confidential Compute Apps through private work and contract evaluation, signed minimum-necessary amount, explicit disclosure boundary, machine/attestation label, and onchain verification. Contract Registry is used for protocol addresses.
- **Demo Hook:** A contractor submits a private artifact and the business clicks “Review milestone.” The public screen shows only “60% approved,” releases exactly 60% of the FXRP escrow, and leaves the remainder locked. Change the rubric after submission and the next run visibly stops with “Terms changed—review required.”
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The payments/work cluster shows that clear parties and visible money movement are compelling, but privacy often sits beside escrow rather than controlling it. Its unresolved edge is disputed partial work and mid-stream policy change. The opportunity is an enforceable partial amount with an explicit review branch.
- **Missing Outcome:** Existing business and contractor settle the undisputed portion immediately without exposing the work artifact, invoice, or negotiation, while the disputed remainder stays safely controlled.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: business-controlled XRP/FXRP funding → bounded escrow → partial release or retained remainder → payment receipt. Confidential track: private rubric and work evidence → FCC/FCE entitlement computation → signed amount/status → verifier. No release function succeeds without a current result bound to the escrow and rubric commitment.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: there is no funded escrow or irreversible partial payment, so FairPartial becomes private document review. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: the evidence must be disclosed to the contract or a centralized reviewer, and the privacy-preserving entitlement outcome fails.
- **Proof Path:** Business funds one FXRP escrow → both parties display the same rubric commitment → contractor submits private evidence → FCC/FCE runs with honest status label → verifier accepts the signed 60% entitlement → contract releases exactly 60% → contractor balance and remaining escrow are shown → a post-submission rubric change triggers `REVIEW` with no second transfer.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** Business owns the funds and authorizes the bounded escrow → contractor supplies private evidence for the already agreed relationship → FCC/FCE evaluates the committed rubric but controls no funds → verifier accepts only a signed, current, escrow-bound result → escrow contract releases no more than the business-funded cap to the named existing contractor → receipts go to both parties. Neither the app nor evaluator can enlarge the budget, change the recipient, or invent work.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts private conditional entitlement and milestone escrow around the overlooked middle answer between full payment and denial. First-five-user reach is five existing business-contractor relationships; the product asks no one to join a marketplace or understand confidential-compute terminology.

## 4. StillMe

- **Name:** StillMe
- **Problem:** If a wallet owner loses access or stops responding, money can remain stuck forever. But handing it to a backup person too early can be theft—especially when the wallet service or recovery machine, rather than the owner, is the thing that failed.
- **Market Anchor:** Self-custodied XRP/FXRP holders already depend on wallets, Smart Accounts, executors, and holder-owned accounts. Known edge states include unavailable executors, signer failure, false inactivity, contested beneficiaries, machine loss, and policy migration.
- **Named Buyer:** A self-custodied XRP holder who already uses an XRPL wallet and holder-owned Flare Smart Account.
- **Existing Workflow:** The holder authorizes XRP/FXRP use through the wallet or Smart Account, keeps private recovery instructions separately, and relies on manual support or native recovery controls if access fails.
- **Current Substitute:** Seed backups, informal beneficiary instructions, wallet support, inactivity timers, multisig/custody recovery, and eventual permissionless execution. A simple timer cannot distinguish owner absence from service failure and may release value prematurely.
- **Mechanism:** While active, the holder commits a private recovery policy containing backup recipient, waiting period, contest rules, amount cap, and safe destination, then pre-authorizes only a reversible holding action. Recovery requires two independently visible absences: FDC-confirmed lack of the agreed XRPL owner event during the window and declared non-liveness of the primary executor/service. FCC/FCE privately evaluates the policy and any contest evidence, emitting `REVERSIBLE_HANDOFF`, `CANCEL`, or `WAIT`. The first asset action moves bounded FXRP into a holder-defined recovery holding state; final release occurs only after the challenge window.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** FDC negative evidence about the XRPL owner and a Flare Smart Account authorization jointly control a reversible FXRP state transition. The two-chain absence condition and staged asset handoff are the product, not a generic password-reset flow.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through FDC absence evidence, Smart Account authorization, and real FXRP hold/cancel/release transitions; Confidential Compute Apps through private beneficiary and contest-policy evaluation, minimum signed result, declared machine/attestation status, and verifier contract. Contract Registry resolves changing protocol addresses.
- **Demo Hook:** A countdown shows “Owner check-in missing,” then the recovery service is also taken offline. Instead of paying a beneficiary immediately, the system moves a small bounded FXRP amount into a reversible holding state. The original owner checks in during the challenge window and one click cancels the handoff, producing a visible return receipt.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The continuity cluster commonly proves user inactivity, but is weaker when the recovery infrastructure itself fails. Its portable insight is dual absence evidence followed by reversible handoff before final release, not a dead-man switch with one irreversible timer.
- **Missing Outcome:** Assets remain recoverable after either owner-access or service failure without turning a false inactivity signal into an immediate irreversible payout.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: XRPL activity/non-activity evidence → FDC attestation → Smart Account-authorized FXRP handoff → challenge cancel or final release → receipts. Confidential track: private beneficiary, limits, and contest rules → FCC/FCE resolution → signed wait/handoff/cancel result → verifier. The verifier requires both external absence evidence and a current private-policy result.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: there is no XRPL absence proof, Smart Account authority, or reversible FXRP consequence, leaving a generic inheritance checklist. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: beneficiary identity, caps, and contest evidence must be public or evaluated by a trusted custodian; private policy-controlled recovery fails.
- **Proof Path:** Holder commits policy and pre-authorizes only the bounded recovery state machine → simulate or stage the agreed XRPL inactivity window with honest evidence labels → declare primary service non-liveness → FDC and service-status inputs reach FCC/FCE → verifier accepts `REVERSIBLE_HANDOFF` → FXRP enters the holding state → owner submits the agreed XRPL check-in during the challenge window → `CANCEL` returns FXRP → exact before/after receipts show no beneficiary payout.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** Holder defines the beneficiary, limits, challenge rule, and pre-authorization while active → FDC attests the agreed XRPL event or its absence but chooses no recipient → service liveness is evidence, not authority → FCC/FCE privately resolves the committed policy and controls no funds → verifier enforces evidence, amount cap, and time window → holder-owned Smart Account moves FXRP only through the pre-authorized reversible states → original holder can cancel under the committed rule before final release.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts dual absence proof, local safety boundaries, and reversible continuity to the plain question “Can I get my money back if the wallet service fails?” First-five-user testing needs only existing wallet holders and small bounded amounts. Negative evidence, FDC delay, and FCC/FCE status remain explicit so the demo does not overclaim production readiness.
