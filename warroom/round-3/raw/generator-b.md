# Generator B — Round 3

## 1. AfterMint

- **Name:** AfterMint
- **Problem:** An XRP holder can complete the source-chain payment after abandoning the mint screen, or repeat an intent while the first proof is delayed. The resulting FXRP may exist, but the holder no longer knows whether it is safe to deploy or whether a second intent conflicts with it.
- **Market Anchor:** Existing XRP holders already use direct mint or Smart Account paths to obtain FXRP, with substantial FXRP supply, millions of FXRP DeFi transactions, high deployment of circulating FXRP, and funded wallet-deposit campaigns. The exposed edge is the payment that arrives after the guided journey has stopped.
- **Named Buyer:** A self-custodied XRP holder using an existing XRPL wallet and a holder-owned Flare Personal Account.
- **Existing Workflow:** The holder initiates direct mint or signs an XRP instruction, waits for proof, receives FXRP, and manually deploys it into an existing strategy.
- **Current Substitute:** Manual preflight, wallet warnings, native recovery controls, support documentation, and checking both chains by hand. These help before or around failure but do not bind a late proof to the holder's original private intent and finish the safe next action.
- **Mechanism:** Before payment, the holder commits an intent containing amount bounds, expiry, intended holder-owned destination, target-strategy preference, and a unique intent nonce. When FDC observes the XRP payment, FCC/FCE compares the attested payment with that private intent and the holder's other open intent nonces. It emits a signed minimum-disclosure result: `DEPLOY_EXACT`, `HOLD_IN_PERSONAL_ACCOUNT`, or `ABSTAIN_DUPLICATE`, plus the matched intent hash and expiry. A Flare contract verifies the result and permits only the pre-authorized Smart Account action; mismatch or uncertainty leaves FXRP in the holder-owned account.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** The product reconciles an XRPL payment, an FDC proof, an FXRP mint state, and an XRPL-authorized Flare Smart Account call. Remove the XRP-to-FXRP lifecycle and there is no interrupted cross-chain intent to recover.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through a real XRP/FDC/FXRP/Smart Account lifecycle; Confidential Compute Apps through necessary private intent reconciliation, explicit minimum disclosure, signed output, machine-status label, and contract verification. Mutable protocol addresses are resolved through Contract Registry.
- **Demo Hook:** Start one mint intent, abandon it during visible FDC latency, then submit a conflicting second intent. When the first proof arrives, the UI shows the raw proof status, FCC/FCE status, signed `HOLD_IN_PERSONAL_ACCOUNT` result, FXRP balance, and an exact refusal receipt for the conflicting deployment.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The asset-entry cluster makes happy-path minting legible, but safe intervention after proof delay, duplicated intent, or user abandonment remains weak. The reusable insight is intent commitment followed by exact reconciliation, not another mint wizard.
- **Missing Outcome:** A late or duplicated mint finishes in a safe holder-controlled state without exposing portfolio context or relying on support to reconstruct intent.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: XRPL payment → FDC attestation → FXRP mint state → holder-authorized Smart Account hold/deploy action. Confidential track: private intent and portfolio constraints → FCC/FCE reconciliation → signed three-way result → verifier contract. The tracks join because the verifier result selects the only allowed disposition of the newly minted FXRP.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: there is no cross-chain payment proof, FXRP consequence, or Smart Account authorization, so AfterMint becomes a generic task reconciler. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: the contract cannot safely distinguish late, duplicate, and deployable intent without publishing the holder's amount bounds, strategy preference, and portfolio context, so automatic deployment must stop.
- **Proof Path:** Holder commits intent and signs the XRP instruction → XRP payment lands → FDC proof becomes visible after its real delay → FXRP mint state is shown → FCC/FCE evaluates the proof against private intent → verifier accepts the signed result and declared machine status → Smart Account holds or deploys exactly the bounded amount → transaction links and before/after balances show the consequence; a duplicate run shows the refusal safeguard.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** Holder supplies private intent and signs the XRP instruction → FDC attests only the external payment → FCC/FCE may classify but cannot move funds → verifier contract accepts only a result tied to the holder's commitment and current expiry → the holder's controller/Personal Account executes the already authorized call into an existing application or retains FXRP → receipt. The product never controls native FAssets assignment or capacity.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts the pre-action/post-action guardian and external-fact trigger into a post-abandonment recovery mechanic. The fresh move is making duplicate detection and safe holding the product outcome, with honest `LIVE`, `SIMULATED`, `REGISTERED`, or `ATTESTED` labels rather than claiming unavailable FCC production guarantees.

## 2. Remainder Rail

- **Name:** Remainder Rail
- **Problem:** A registered FAssets agent can partially pay an assigned redemption and then face a proof delay, liquidity mismatch, stale collateral view, or unavailable work signer. A rushed corrective payment may worsen exposure; a dashboard diagnosis still leaves the redemption unfinished.
- **Market Anchor:** Registered agents earn mint/redemption fee shares while maintaining locked collateral and bearing liquidation, default, and challenge exposure. Repeated historical agent activity makes redemption exception handling a current operational workflow rather than a hypothetical market.
- **Named Buyer:** A registered FAssets agent's treasury or operations lead.
- **Existing Workflow:** The agent posts collateral, accepts protocol-assigned demand, manages XRP liquidity, fulfills assigned redemptions, proves payments, and monitors collateral health.
- **Current Substitute:** Agent console, internal treasury tools, public monitoring, and native proof/default/liquidation mechanisms. These expose status or punish failure, but do not privately test whether a specific remainder payment is safe and then complete it within the agent's existing authority.
- **Mechanism:** For an already assigned redemption, FDC derives what XRP payment is present or absent. FCC/FCE privately evaluates the agent's liquidity position, internal exposure limit, emergency policy, and planned movement against two bounded counterfactuals: pay the exact remainder now or abstain. It reveals only `PAY_REMAINDER(amount, assignmentHash, expiry)` or `ABSTAIN(reasonCode)`, signed under a declared machine/attestation state. A verifier contract accepts the result; the agent's own authorized wallet performs the XRP payment, and FDC proof closes the existing redemption lifecycle.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** The decision is anchored to a native FAssets redemption assignment, external XRP payment evidence, FDC proof, and the resulting redemption/default state. It cannot be replaced by moving an arbitrary token because the proof window and corrective payment belong to the XRP-backed FAsset lifecycle.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through an actual FXRP redemption exception and FDC-confirmed XRP completion; Confidential Compute Apps through private treasury counterfactuals, signed bounded authorization, explicit disclosure boundary, attestation/status label, and onchain verification. Contract Registry resolves FAssets addresses.
- **Demo Hook:** Inject a deterministic partial-payment case. The first screen shows the assigned amount and FDC-attested paid amount; the private simulation then authorizes only the exact remainder. The agent-owned wallet pays it, a new FDC proof arrives, and a before/after receipt changes the case from partial to completed. A second case visibly abstains when the internal bound would be exceeded.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The FAssets assurance cluster is strong at diagnosis, insurance, and negative evidence but weak at completing a policy-bounded recovery. Counterfactual simulation becomes valuable only when it authorizes the exact native corrective action.
- **Missing Outcome:** A partial redemption reaches completion—or an explicit safe abstention—without publishing the agent's liquidity plan and without allowing the app to invent or alter protocol assignments.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: existing redemption assignment → FDC presence/absence evidence → agent-owned XRP remainder payment → FDC proof → FAssets completion state. Confidential track: private liquidity/exposure/emergency inputs → FCC/FCE counterfactual → signed exact-amount authorization or abstention → verifier. The signed result is the mandatory gate for the agent's corrective-payment workflow.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: there is no assignment, proof window, external payment, or redemption completion, leaving generic treasury simulation. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: either sensitive liquidity policy is published or the remainder is paid without the counterfactual safety guarantee; the bounded recovery outcome fails.
- **Proof Path:** Select a real or clearly labeled deterministic assigned-redemption scenario → show FDC-derived paid/absent evidence and freshness → commit private treasury inputs → run FCC/FCE with explicit status → verify signed authorization onchain → agent-owned wallet pays the exact XRP remainder → obtain FDC proof → show completed redemption state, transaction references, and an over-limit abstention replay.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** Native FAssets protocol assigns the redemption → agent treasury supplies private policy and retains its keys → FDC attests payment evidence but grants no authority → FCC/FCE recommends an exact bounded action but cannot transact → verifier checks assignment hash, amount, policy version, and expiry → agent-owned wallet authorizes the XRP payment → published FAssets interface processes the proof → receipt. The application neither accepts demand nor changes capacity or assignment.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts closed-loop diagnosis/action and confidential counterfactual simulation to the overlooked remainder-payment edge. The design treats proof delay and uncertainty as explicit abstention states and stages FDC latency visibly instead of hiding it.

## 3. Relay Epoch

- **Name:** Relay Epoch
- **Problem:** A Smart Account executor can lose its machine, rotate credentials, or recover after downtime while an owner-signed XRP instruction is still pending. The signature may remain valid even though the payload, nonce, fee, policy version, or target conditions are now stale; blindly relaying it is unsafe, while doing nothing strands the authorized action.
- **Market Anchor:** Existing Smart Account operators and wallet integrations have wallet routes into live applications and can earn executor fees. Their known operational failures include payload mismatch, stale nonce, inadequate fee, unavailable executor, failed target calls, and credential compromise.
- **Named Buyer:** An existing Smart Account operator/executor or XRPL wallet integration.
- **Existing Workflow:** Observe an owner-signed XRP instruction, obtain proof, deliver the committed call bytes, and relay execution through the controller/Personal Account into an existing application or vault.
- **Current Substitute:** Backend preflight, serialization checks, monitoring, recovery controls, and eventual permissionless execution. These can find faults or change the relay, but they do not prove that a recovered executor is using the same private call commitment under the current policy epoch.
- **Mechanism:** Each owner-signed instruction binds a call hash, nonce, expiry, and policy epoch. After executor loss or rotation, FCC/FCE privately receives the committed call bytes, operator credentials, availability policy, and risk thresholds; it simulates the exact call against the current epoch and emits a signed `RELAY_EXACT` or `REFUSE_STALE` result containing only hashes, epoch, expiry, and reason code. The verifier contract checks the result and the Smart Account proof before allowing any executor to deliver the immutable call bytes. Failed or unavailable confidential compute produces abstention, not fallback execution.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** The recovery preserves an XRPL owner's authorization across Flare Smart Account proof and Personal Account execution. The executor never receives authority to substitute a call, which is specific to the cross-chain authorization path rather than a generic relayer queue.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through a live Smart Account authorization and target asset action; Confidential Compute Apps through private exact-payload simulation, machine/attestation status, signed epoch result, minimum disclosure, and verifier contract. An FXRP deposit, withdrawal, or allocation in an existing application supplies the judge-visible asset consequence.
- **Demo Hook:** Kill the first executor after an XRP instruction is signed, rotate to a second declared machine epoch, and attempt relay twice. A changed byte or stale policy yields a public refusal receipt; the exact committed payload passes private simulation and the replacement executor completes the FXRP action with the same owner authorization.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The treasury/signer cluster separates key possession from policy, but recovery, machine rotation, degraded mode, and valid-yet-stale intent remain weak. The continuity cluster adds the stronger outcome: infrastructure failure must end in a reversible, authority-preserving handoff rather than another alert.
- **Missing Outcome:** A pending owner-authorized asset action survives executor failure without granting the replacement executor broader authority and without executing a now-invalid intent.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: XRPL owner signature → proof → Smart Account/controller → existing FXRP application call → receipt. Confidential track: private payload/credential/policy check on the replacement machine → signed current-epoch result → contract verification. The Smart Account call is executable only when both the owner proof and confidential epoch result agree on the same call hash.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: Relay Epoch becomes ordinary job failover with no XRPL authorization or Flare asset consequence. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: replacement execution cannot validate private bytes and operator policy without disclosure, so safe machine rotation and the stale-intent guarantee disappear.
- **Proof Path:** Owner signs one XRP instruction → first executor receives it and is made unavailable → second machine declares its status and epoch → FDC/Smart Account proof is obtained → FCC/FCE simulates the private committed bytes → verifier rejects a mutated/stale attempt → verifier accepts the exact current attempt → replacement executor relays → target FXRP state and transaction receipt are shown.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** XRPL owner alone authorizes the instruction → proof binds that authority to the controller/Personal Account → operator supplies private relay material but cannot change call bytes → FCC/FCE evaluates and signs only a bounded result → verifier checks owner proof, call hash, nonce, epoch, and expiry → any eligible executor relays the immutable call → Personal Account invokes the existing application/vault → receipt. Rotation changes service availability, not owner authority.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts policy/machine rotation, forced policy expiry, and local safety boundaries into a two-key condition: owner authorization plus current confidential execution fitness. Honest status labels expose whether the FCC/FCE leg is simulated, registered, or attested, and the demo includes refusal rather than masking downtime.

## 4. LedgerTwin

- **Name:** LedgerTwin
- **Problem:** An XRP-funded business can receive a source-chain payment and later issue an FXRP settlement for the same invoice twice because the first payment was late, partially interpreted, or reconciled under a different internal reference. Public chain data shows transfers, but not whether two transfers satisfy the same private commercial obligation.
- **Market Anchor:** Crypto-native SMEs and payment operators already controlling XRP have active B2B settlement workflows. Wrong recipients, duplicate payments, exposed future intent, policy breaches, and liquidity mismatch are documented pains; the concept is admitted only for an evidenced XRP-funded pilot, not an invented marketplace.
- **Named Buyer:** A crypto-native SME treasury lead or payment operator that already controls the participating XRP and holder-owned Flare account.
- **Existing Workflow:** Receive XRP, map it privately to a counterparty and invoice, approve a settlement, convert or deploy assets, pay from the treasury, and reconcile the receipt in private accounting software.
- **Current Substitute:** Private accounting/payment software, manual approvals, multisig or custody controls, allowlists, and manual conversion. These know the invoice or control the wallet, but they do not cryptographically join an FDC-attested XRP receipt to the exact private obligation before a Smart Account releases FXRP.
- **Mechanism:** The treasury commits a salted obligation key derived from its private invoice/purpose, counterparty map, amount bounds, and approval policy. FDC attests the relevant XRP payment. FCC/FCE privately tests whether that evidence already consumes all or part of the obligation and emits a signed `RELEASE_FXRP(amount, obligationHash, expiry)`, `ALREADY_SETTLED`, or `REVIEW_PARTIAL` result. A verifier contract allows the treasury's pre-authorized Smart Account to release only the approved FXRP amount; the post-action receipt is folded into the same obligation state to prevent replay.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** The product closes a specific XRP funding → FDC evidence → FXRP release → Smart Account receipt loop. FDC-attested XRP settlement is not treated as a generic oracle datum: it consumes the one private obligation that gates the paired FXRP action and replay protection.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Interoperable Asset Products through FDC-attested XRP funding and a real holder-authorized FXRP payment; Confidential Compute Apps through private invoice/counterparty/approval evaluation, minimal signed status, declared machine state, and contract verification. Contract Registry supplies mutable protocol addresses.
- **Demo Hook:** Send one XRP payment late, then present two differently labeled settlement requests that secretly reference the same invoice. The first valid request releases bounded FXRP; the second returns `ALREADY_SETTLED` with the same commitment but no invoice disclosure. A partial-payment variant visibly routes to `REVIEW_PARTIAL` and moves no funds.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight (anonymized cluster only):** The payments cluster demonstrates visible money movement, but privacy and settlement often sit beside one another rather than being causally linked. The external-evidence cluster similarly tends to stop at a label or alert. The missing join is a private obligation decision that consumes verified XRP evidence and directly gates one FXRP release.
- **Missing Outcome:** The treasury finishes cross-ledger reconciliation and prevents a duplicate FXRP settlement without publishing invoice terms, counterparty identity, approval graph, or internal balance.
- **Multi-Track Architecture:** Interoperable track: treasury-controlled XRP receipt → FDC attestation → Smart Account-authorized FXRP release → receipt. Confidential track: private obligation and approval state → FCC/FCE consumption check → signed release/already-settled/review result → verifier. The verifier maintains only commitment consumption state; the private result is causally required for every release.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Interoperable Asset Products: there is no verified XRP funding event, FXRP consequence, or cross-ledger replay to prevent, leaving generic invoice software. Remove Confidential Compute Apps: invoice identity and approval state must be exposed or the contract cannot know that differently labeled requests are one obligation, so privacy-preserving duplicate prevention fails.
- **Proof Path:** Treasury commits one private obligation → XRP payment occurs → FDC proof and freshness are displayed → FCC/FCE evaluates it with private invoice and approval data → verifier accepts the signed result → treasury-authorized Smart Account releases exact FXRP → receipt marks the obligation commitment consumed → replay under another label is refused; partial evidence shows a no-transfer review branch.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** Treasury controls the source XRP, supplies the private obligation state, and signs the allowed Smart Account action → FDC attests the external payment only → FCC/FCE computes a decision but holds no treasury authority → verifier binds result to obligation hash, amount, policy version, and expiry → holder-owned controller/Personal Account releases FXRP to the treasury's existing counterparty → receipt updates the commitment state. No invented exchange crediting, custodian authority, or third-party asset control is assumed.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts private conditional entitlement, external-fact triggering, and post-action guarding to the duplicate-settlement edge. Unlike ordinary backend privacy, the private result is contract-verified and mandatory for the FXRP release; unlike a token-interchangeable invoice app, the demonstrated state machine begins with FDC-attested XRP and ends with XRPL-authorized FXRP settlement.
