# Generator I — Novel DeFi State Machine and Verifiable Private Decision

IDEA 1: REDEMPTION LADDER
Problem: An FXRP holder facing uncertain redemption capacity must choose between waiting blindly, accepting a full loss of liquidity, or revealing urgency and minimum recovery terms to an operator who can exploit them.
Mechanism: A confidential redemption ladder moves escrowed FXRP through WAIT, PARTIAL EXIT, and FULL EXIT states, releasing only the largest privately acceptable tranche that current verified capacity can safely satisfy.
Chain-Native Angle: The economic state machine is native to the FAsset exit lifecycle: real FXRP is locked, partially redeemed or restored on Flare, while private urgency and loss tolerance determine which transition is permitted.
Sponsor Fit: A real FXRP escrow/redemption/refund transition is the load-bearing Interoperable Asset Products primitive; FCC/FCE privately evaluates the holder's minimum recovery, deadline, tranche preferences, agent-capacity inputs, and vault safety bounds, signs one ladder transition, and supplies the result the ladder contract must verify before moving value.
Demo Hook: The judge lowers available capacity below a hidden full-exit threshold; the position visibly steps from WAIT to PARTIAL EXIT, releases exactly 40 FXRP, then a second run with unsafe capacity produces ABSTAIN and leaves the remainder recoverable.
Competitor-Derived Insight: FAssets assurance systems diagnose redemption risk and confidential trading hides execution intent, but few convert a private tolerance into a staged, failure-safe exit rather than an all-or-nothing recommendation.
Missing Outcome: A privacy-preserving partial-redemption path that preserves the holder's remaining claim and never worsens exposure when capacity is thin or stale.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: real FXRP lock, partial release/redemption, and remainder refund transition → turn uncertain exit capacity into a recoverable staged position
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE private tranche-selection and counterfactual safety computation → reveal only wait/partial/full/abstain, executable amount, state commitment, and expiry
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the private decision cannot retire, release, or restore actual FXRP, so no economic exit state changes
  - Remove confidential primitive: urgency, loss tolerance, and safety constraints become public or the contract cannot prove the chosen tranche satisfies the holder's private bounds
Proof Path: holder deposits FXRP and requests an exit → contract shows locked balance and current lifecycle/capacity evidence → FCC/FCE privately simulates wait, partial, and full transitions → signed least-worsening transition is contract-verified → exact FXRP tranche moves and remainder advances or refunds → judge receives deposit, machine-status, signed-decision, transition, balance-delta, and remaining-claim receipts; stale capacity, zero safe tranche, or replay produces abstention with funds unchanged
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are urgency, minimum recovery, tranche schedule, deadline, private capacity data, and vault reserve floor; revealed output is state action, amount cap, remaining-position commitment, reason class, nonce, and expiry. The UI displays FCC/FCE machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status; the contract verifies signer, position hash, state version, amount, nonce, and deadline. If a production redemption call is unavailable, the adapter and capacity input are explicitly labeled simulated while FXRP escrow transitions remain onchain.
Build Slice: One FXRP ladder contract plus an optional redemption adapter, one deterministic FCC/FCE tranche function, and a Next.js position card with full, partial, stale, and replay scenarios; no more than two contracts.
Adaptation Note: novel DeFi state machine + verifiable private decision + closed-loop diagnosis and action (cross-pollination) → private risk tolerance selects a staged FAsset exit with a preserved remainder instead of a binary redemption.

IDEA 2: SHADOW DRAWDOWN VAULT
Problem: An FXRP saver with a sensitive emergency-loss threshold must either publish the trigger and invite anticipatory trading or trust an automation bot with durable withdrawal authority that may execute after prices, routes, or policy have changed.
Mechanism: A Smart Account gives a confidential drawdown policy one expiring transition from INVESTED to BUFFERED, executing a bounded FXRP unwind only if the private loss and liquidity conditions are simultaneously met.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare Smart Account authorization makes a hidden personal risk boundary enforceable over real FXRP and makes the unwind authority disappear after the single state transition.
Sponsor Fit: Smart Account authorization is load-bearing because it grants and consumes the precise permission to withdraw FXRP from a demo strategy vault into a protected buffer; FCC/FCE privately evaluates entry value, current loss, emergency threshold, liquidity need, route slippage, and policy version, signs unwind/hold/abstain, and lets the account module verify the result before acting.
Demo Hook: The judge moves a public price slider while the trigger remains hidden; at the exact confidential boundary the vault flips from INVESTED to BUFFERED and FXRP unwinds, then a replay visibly fails because the transition capability has expired.
Competitor-Derived Insight: Wallet automation makes allocation easy and private conditional execution protects strategy, but recovery after failed automation and causal private control of a unique asset action remain underserved.
Missing Outcome: A one-shot confidential stop policy that completes a safe unwind without exposing the threshold or leaving a bot with permanent control.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: Smart Account single-use authorization → execute and finalize one real FXRP strategy-to-buffer transition
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE private drawdown and route-policy evaluation → reveal only unwind/hold/abstain, amount cap, route commitment, policy version, and expiry
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the confidential trigger cannot safely compel or constrain the saver-owned unwind, leaving durable bot custody or no action
  - Remove confidential primitive: the stop threshold and liquidity need become public, or the Smart Account receives an unverifiable automation instruction
Proof Path: saver arms a committed stop policy and deposits FXRP → Smart Account and vault expose INVESTED state plus observable valuation input → FCC/FCE privately evaluates drawdown, need, and executable route → signed one-use transition is verified by the account → authorized FXRP withdraws into the buffer and capability burns → judge receives policy commitment, machine status, signed result, unwind transaction, before/after balances, and rejected replay; stale valuation, excessive slippage, or policy mismatch causes hold/abstain with no movement
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are entry basis, drawdown threshold, cash target, route preference, slippage cap, and emergency flag; revealed output is action, amount cap, route commitment, version, nonce, and expiry. Machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status are shown; the Smart Account module verifies FCC/FCE signer, state hash, policy version, bounds, nonce, and deadline. Strategy yield and route quotes may use labeled fixtures, while authorization consumption and FXRP unwind are contract-verifiable.
Build Slice: One minimal Smart Account module and one FXRP strategy/buffer vault, one FCC/FCE policy function, and a slider-driven UI with hold, unwind, stale-route, and replay tests; two contracts total.
Adaptation Note: novel DeFi state machine + policy-controlled autonomous execution + forced memory scarcity (cross-pollination) → a hidden stop-loss becomes a one-transition authority that forgets how to move funds once the saver reaches safety.

IDEA 3: PROOF WATERFALL
Problem: An FXRP recovery reserve handling a redemption shortfall can pay the first claimant too much when external payments arrive late or conflict, leaving later valid claimants with nothing and exposing every claimant's evidence and priority details.
Mechanism: A confidential claim waterfall uses FDC-verified external fulfillment to advance each incident through PENDING, PATCHED, DEFERRED, or REFUNDED, allocating only the provable residual shortfall under a private reserve-priority policy.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare uniquely joins XRPL fulfillment evidence to an ordered FXRP recovery pool, allowing external payment changes to recompute—not merely report—the next permissible asset transition.
Sponsor Fit: The load-bearing interoperable primitive is the FDC-verified redemption-payment lifecycle that establishes paid, partial, absent, or late fulfillment before any FXRP reserve movement; FCC/FCE privately evaluates claimant evidence, agent exposure, competing claims, reserve floor, and priority policy, signs the next waterfall transition, and supplies a result the recovery contract verifies before payout or deferral.
Demo Hook: The judge injects a late external payment for Claim A; its patch shrinks from 30 to 10 FXRP and the freed 20 immediately advances Claim B from DEFERRED to PATCHED, with both before/after receipts visible but private rankings hidden.
Competitor-Derived Insight: Risk products value negative evidence and explicit abstention, while credit systems show signed private terms can control capital; the missing economic outcome is fair reprioritization when external evidence changes after a recovery queue forms.
Missing Outcome: A privacy-preserving, evidence-responsive recovery waterfall that prevents double compensation and protects later claimants when proof is late or contradictory.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: FDC-verified external redemption fulfillment and one-time FXRP reserve transitions → calculate residual shortfall and move scarce recovery value through an ordered claim lifecycle
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE private residual, priority, and reserve-safety computation → reveal only patched/deferred/refunded/abstain, capped amounts, queue-root update, and reason classes
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the pool cannot prove external fulfillment or residual loss, so payouts can double-compensate fabricated or already-paid claims
  - Remove confidential primitive: claimant circumstances, agent exposure, competing claims, and priority rules become public, or an operator can secretly favor a claimant
Proof Path: operator submits or refreshes a claim → FDC establishes external payment presence, absence, or partial amount against current reserve state → FCC/FCE privately recomputes residuals and the next safe waterfall transition → signed queue-root and payout vector are contract-verified → FXRP patches eligible claims or preserves funds in deferred state → judge receives FDC evidence status, old/new queue roots, machine/attestation receipt, signed result, payout transactions, reserve delta, and duplicate rejection; conflicting, stale, or over-reserve evidence forces abstention and freezes the affected branch only
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are claimant evidence, loss details, agent exposure, competing claim amounts, hardship/priority attributes, and reserve thresholds; revealed output is per-claim action and cap, aggregate reserve delta, old/new queue roots, coarse reason, nonce, and expiry. The UI labels FCC/FCE machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status; the contract verifies signer, FDC evidence commitments, root transition, payout sum, nonces, and deadline. Seeded claims or unavailable FDC proofs are labeled fixtures/simulated, while queue-root verification and FXRP payouts are onchain.
Build Slice: One FXRP waterfall reserve and one evidence/result verifier, one FCC/FCE allocation function for two claims, and a Next.js queue demo covering late payment, partial patch, contradiction, and replay; two contracts total.
Adaptation Note: novel DeFi state machine + external fact as state-transition trigger + closed-loop diagnosis and action (cross-pollination) → late proof dynamically reallocates a scarce recovery waterfall while preserving private priority inputs.
