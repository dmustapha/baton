IDEA 1: FORGET-TO-REDEEM
Problem: An FXRP holder redeeming through a service that privately stores route, identity, and fallback data cannot prove those sensitive instructions were erased after settlement, so stale recovery material can later authorize an unwanted reroute or leak.
Mechanism: A redemption cannot finalize until the FCC/FCE machine destroys the one-use private instruction capsule and signs an erasure receipt bound to the exact FDC-confirmed external payout.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare can make an XRPL payout fact and an FXRP redemption consequence depend on a verifiable confidential erasure event, turning deletion from a service promise into a lifecycle transition.
Sponsor Fit: A real FXRP redemption plus FDC verification proves the external XRP payout; FCC/FCE holds the private payout route, identity mapping, and fallback secret, deletes that capsule after use, and signs ERASED(capsuleCommitment, payoutHash, machineEpoch), which the redemption contract verifies before closing the request and releasing the agent bond.
Demo Hook: Redeem FXRP, show XRP arrive, then watch the request remain economically open until “Erase” produces a signed receipt; replaying the deleted capsule fails while the bond unlocks.
Competitor-Derived Insight: The FAssets exception cluster values before/after receipts, while protected-data products leave downstream erasure weakly handled; joining them makes privacy completion a real asset state.
Missing Outcome: The user gets proof that settlement instructions cannot survive redemption to enable later misuse, including a safeguard when deletion cannot be attested.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: real FXRP redemption with FDC-confirmed XRP payout → completes the cross-chain asset exit and identifies the exact private capsule that must be retired
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE one-use capsule execution and signed erasure operation → makes bond release and redemption finality conditional on deletion
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: there is no real redemption or external payout to bind the erasure to, so deletion has no user-valued asset consequence
  - Remove confidential primitive: private route material can persist after payout and the contract cannot safely close redemption or release the agent bond
Proof Path: holder submits FXRP redemption and capsule commitment → real FXRP enters redemption and FDC verifies the corresponding XRP payout → identified FCC/FCE machine uses then erases the private route capsule → signed ERASED result is verified by the redemption contract → request closes and agent bond releases, or remains locked on erasure failure → judge sees FXRP burn/request receipt, XRP transaction hash, FDC status, machine identity/attestation label, erasure signature, bond release, and a rejected replay.
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are XRPL destination details, identity mapping, fallback route, and recovery secret; revealed output is capsule commitment, payout hash, erasure status, and machine epoch. The UI shows machine identity, code hash, and live/registered/attested/simulated status; the contract verifies signature, payout binding, epoch, and one-use nullifier. The demo states that signed logical deletion proves execution of the measured erasure routine, not physical destruction beyond the represented machine boundary, and labels any fixture payout or simulated attestation.
Build Slice: One redemption-finalizer/bond contract, one FXRP redemption or honest fixture adapter, one FCC/FCE capsule routine, and two scripted paths: erase-and-release and replay rejection.
Adaptation Note: Pre-action and post-action guardian + privacy-preserving value flow → redemption remains unfinished until its private residue is removed; cross-pollination: machine unlearning / erasure proof supplies the irreversible “erase before bond release” rule.

IDEA 2: ONE-SHOT TREASURY
Problem: A small XRPL treasury using confidential standing policy can silently accumulate stale permissions, so a compromised Smart Account or operator may reuse yesterday’s private approval for a new FXRP transfer.
Mechanism: Every approved policy fact is consumed forever by one Smart Account asset action, forcing the treasury to re-authorize from a fresh private state before any subsequent transfer.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare Smart Account authorization can enforce policy amnesia on real FXRP movement while FCC/FCE evaluates sensitive limits and beneficiaries without publishing them.
Sponsor Fit: Smart Account authorization performs the real FXRP transfer; FCC/FCE receives the private beneficiary allowlist, daily exposure, purpose, and retained exception, signs CONSUME(actionHash, policyEpoch, nullifier, cap), then erases the decision context, and the account verifies and burns the nullifier before executing.
Demo Hook: Approve one FXRP payment, execute it, and immediately replay the same perfectly valid signature; the second transfer irreversibly fails until a fresh private authorization creates a new epoch.
Competitor-Derived Insight: Treasury-policy systems prove bounded autonomy, but stale signer sets and valid approvals for now-invalid intent remain unserved; forced policy expiry makes that edge state the central mechanic.
Missing Outcome: Asset owners receive non-reusable confidential authorization with an exact exhaustion receipt instead of relying on revocation after a permission has already leaked.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: Smart Account authorization controlling a real FXRP transfer → turns consumption of a private approval into the necessary gate for value movement
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE fresh evaluation of hidden beneficiary, purpose, exposure, and exception state with signed one-use nullifier → prevents reuse without revealing the policy
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: consuming an approval controls no real asset action, so the product is merely a private policy demo
  - Remove confidential primitive: the account cannot derive a fresh approval from hidden rules, so it must expose them or accept reusable public permissions
Proof Path: operator proposes an FXRP transfer → Smart Account locks the exact action hash and current balance → attested FCC/FCE machine forgets prior working context, evaluates freshly supplied private policy, and signs a capped one-use authorization → account verifies signature and burns nullifier before execution → FXRP transfers once and identical replay reverts → judge sees policy epoch commitment, machine/status label, signed result, nullifier-consumed event, transfer transaction, new balances, and replay-failure receipt.
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are allowlist, purpose, exposure history, and emergency exception; revealed output is action hash, cap, epoch, nullifier, and approve/deny code. Machine identity, measured code/policy commitment, and attestation status are explicit; contract verification checks signer, epoch, cap, nonce, and nullifier uniqueness. The retained exception is committed but not disclosed, and simulated confidential execution is labeled rather than implied to be production TEE security.
Build Slice: One minimal Smart Account with nullifier storage, one FXRP transfer path, one FCC/FCE policy function, and a UI that executes once, proves replay failure, and refreshes to a new epoch.
Adaptation Note: Policy-controlled autonomous execution + verifiable private decision → hidden treasury rules yield a public one-use transfer right; cross-pollination: forced memory scarcity makes “consume before execute” the irreversible product rule.

IDEA 3: LINEAGE TOLL
Problem: A contractor who privately contributes research, code, or a route to an XRP-funded deliverable can be removed from later payment versions because the buyer controls the visible invoice while the valuable contribution remains secret.
Mechanism: Before any PMW XRP payment can be signed, FCC/FCE must derive and seal a confidential contribution lineage; every future derivative payment irreversibly inherits the committed royalty split unless the contributor supplies a signed revocation.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare can bind private provenance to a real XRPL payment action through PMW, making lineage an enforceable cross-chain value rule rather than a public attribution badge.
Sponsor Fit: PMW performs the real external XRP payment; FCC/FCE privately compares the deliverable, predecessor commitment, contributor shares, and revocations, signs LINEAGE(paymentIntent, root, recipients, amounts, epoch), and a verifier contract accepts only that result before PMW signs the corresponding split payment action.
Demo Hook: Clone a private deliverable into version two while attempting to remove the original contributor; the machine detects inherited lineage and PMW visibly pays the contributor’s XRP royalty anyway, while a tampered lineage root produces no signature.
Competitor-Derived Insight: Protected-work products can release payment after private verification, while provenance systems make lineage valuable; the missing outcome is preserving downstream payment rights without exposing the contribution itself.
Missing Outcome: A private contributor receives enforceable value from a derivative external payment, including revocation and lineage-tampering edge states, without a marketplace or public artifact disclosure.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: PMW external-chain XRP split payment → delivers the inherited royalty as an actual external asset consequence
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE private lineage derivation over deliverables, predecessor graph, share terms, and revocations → makes the correct recipient split causally necessary for signing
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: lineage produces no real downstream royalty payment, so attribution has no enforceable economic outcome
  - Remove confidential primitive: PMW cannot prove inheritance or compute the split without exposing the proprietary contribution, so derivative payment is blocked or contributor rights disappear
Proof Path: buyer submits a derivative payment intent and artifact commitments → PMW stages the real XRPL payment but cannot sign it yet → identified FCC/FCE machine privately checks predecessor lineage, contribution match, and revocation state → verifier contract validates signed LINEAGE result and marks the intent executable → PMW signs/broadcasts the prescribed XRP split and FDC confirms settlement → judge sees predecessor and derivative commitments, lineage root, machine/attestation label, result signature, XRPL contributor payment, FDC receipt, and a tampered-root refusal.
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are deliverables, contribution graph, split terms, contributor identity mapping, and revocations; revealed output is lineage root, payment intent, recipient payout instructions needed for settlement, amounts, and epoch. Machine identity/code hash and registered/attested/simulated status are shown; the contract verifies signature, root continuity, epoch, and exact payment commitment. The demo does not claim semantic authorship beyond the deterministic comparison implemented, and any simulated PMW/FDC or TEE element is clearly labeled.
Build Slice: One lineage-verifier contract, one PMW XRP split-payment script, one FCC/FCE deterministic artifact-lineage routine, and three committed fixtures for original, valid derivative, and tampered derivative.
Adaptation Note: Privacy-preserving value flow + stake-backed public claim → a private provenance claim controls a real royalty split; cross-pollination: royalty and lineage plus machine erasure/revocation create the irreversible “seal lineage before external payment” rule.
