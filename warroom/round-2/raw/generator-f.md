# Generator F — Physical-to-Digital Routing and Failure Capsules

IDEA 1: SEALED DELIVERY SWITCH
Problem: An XRP-funded merchant and courier resolving a failed delivery must expose the customer's address, delivery instructions, and dispute photos to a payment operator, while ambiguous arrival evidence can wrongly pay the courier or strand the customer's funds.
Mechanism: A delivery failure capsule privately evaluates committed handoff evidence and routes a real FXRP escrow to courier, merchant, or customer with only a signed outcome and reason class revealed.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare turns externally funded value into programmable FXRP and makes a confidential judgment about a physical handoff causally control the asset's final recipient.
Sponsor Fit: The load-bearing interoperable primitive is a real FXRP escrow release/refund transition funded from an XRP-origin payment lifecycle; FCC/FCE privately evaluates recipient code, timestamp, coarse location, delivery photo hash, courier proof, and merchant rules, signs a minimum-necessary route result, and the escrow contract verifies it before moving FXRP.
Demo Hook: The judge clicks INJECT: WRONG HANDOFF CODE; a funded delivery flips from IN TRANSIT to REFUNDED, FXRP returns to the customer, and a compact receipt proves the confidential machine chose the branch without displaying the address or photo.
Competitor-Derived Insight: Programmable payment products make settlement intuitive, and protected-work systems make private evidence meaningful; the weakly served edge is a disputed physical milestone where confidential verification must cause, not merely accompany, the payout.
Missing Outcome: One-session, privacy-preserving resolution of failed last-mile delivery using an existing buyer-merchant-courier relationship rather than a new marketplace.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: real FXRP fund, release, or refund transition → deliver the escrowed value to the party selected by the verified physical outcome
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE private handoff-policy execution → reveal only courier/merchant/customer route, capped amount, evidence root, reason class, and expiry
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the confidential decision cannot settle or restore the externally sourced asset, so the delivery dispute remains unpaid
  - Remove confidential primitive: customer location, handoff secret, images, and merchant policy must become public or be trusted to a human arbitrator, destroying private deterministic resolution
Proof Path: merchant opens a funded order and judge injects a handoff event → real FXRP escrow state and source-payment reference are displayed → FCC/FCE privately checks committed physical evidence and routing policy → signed route result is verified by the escrow → FXRP releases or refunds to the chosen party → judge receives scenario ID, payment/escrow links, evidence commitment, machine-status and signed-result receipt, asset balance deltas, plus no-transfer abstention for missing or conflicting evidence
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are address, handoff code, delivery photo hash, courier trace, timestamp, and merchant exception rules; revealed output is recipient class, amount cap, evidence root, coarse reason, nonce, and expiry. The UI shows FCC/FCE machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status, and the contract verifies signer, order hash, evidence root, nonce, amount, and deadline. Physical evidence is a deterministic fixture labeled simulated; confidential evaluation and FXRP release/refund are the verifiable demo path.
Build Slice: One FXRP escrow/router contract, one FCC/FCE decision function, and a Next.js order card with delivered, wrong-code, conflicting-evidence, and timeout capsules; one contract total.
Adaptation Note: privacy-preserving value flow + pre-action and post-action guardian + physical-to-digital routing (cross-pollination) → a private physical handoff becomes a deterministic three-way FAsset routing switch.

IDEA 2: COLD-CHAIN MAKE-GOOD
Problem: A small food buyer receiving a temperature-damaged shipment must reveal supplier pricing, sensor history, and loss tolerances to claim compensation, while the supplier risks paying fabricated or duplicated spoilage claims.
Mechanism: A spoilage failure capsule combines FDC-verified XRP invoice payment with confidential excursion analysis to unlock a precisely capped FXRP make-good or freeze the claim on contradictory evidence.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare can prove that the buyer actually paid on XRPL, privately assess sensitive delivery evidence, and complete a compensating FAsset transition in one joined claim lifecycle.
Sponsor Fit: FDC-verified external payment and claim-lifecycle state are load-bearing because only a proven paid invoice may access the FXRP reserve; FCC/FCE privately evaluates the temperature series, permitted excursion, shipment window, item sensitivity, supplier schedule, and prior-claim set, signs pay/deny/freeze with a cap, and provides the only result the reserve contract accepts.
Demo Hook: The judge drags one temperature point above its hidden tolerance; the claim changes from ELIGIBLE to PAID and 18 FXRP moves from the reserve, then replaying the same sensor capsule visibly fails as ALREADY CONSUMED.
Competitor-Derived Insight: External-evidence systems provide trustworthy triggers and payment escrows provide visible money movement, but many stop at diagnosis instead of converting a physical failure into a bounded, reconciled asset correction.
Missing Outcome: Confidential automatic make-good for a paid physical shipment, including duplicate, late, and contradictory-evidence safeguards.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: FDC-verified XRP invoice-payment lifecycle → establish eligibility and permit a one-time FXRP reserve payout
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE private excursion and compensation computation → reveal only pay/deny/freeze, payout cap, evidence commitment, reason class, and expiry
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: a claimant can present sensor data without proving the underlying external payment, so the reserve cannot safely compensate a real purchase
  - Remove confidential primitive: commercially sensitive tolerances, prices, and full sensor traces must be public or an operator can manipulate the compensation amount
Proof Path: buyer selects a paid shipment and injects a temperature capsule → FDC status binds the XRPL invoice payment to an unused claim ID → FCC/FCE privately analyzes the trace and supplier policy → signed compensation result is verified by the reserve → capped FXRP payout executes and consumes the claim → judge receives external-payment status, raw evidence commitment, machine/attestation label, verified result, payout transaction, before/after reserve balance, and replay/stale/conflict refusal receipts
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are temperature series, item class, tolerance curve, purchase price, loss schedule, shipment window, and supplier exception policy; revealed output is action, payout cap, evidence root, coarse reason, nonce, and expiry. Machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status are explicit; the contract verifies the FCC/FCE signature, FDC-linked payment commitment, claim nonce, cap, and expiry. Sensor data and any unavailable FDC response use reproducible fixtures labeled simulated, never live hardware claims; payout verification remains onchain.
Build Slice: One FXRP make-good reserve and one combined evidence/result verifier, one deterministic FCC/FCE excursion function, and a shipment UI with normal, spoiled, duplicate, and contradictory capsules; two contracts total.
Adaptation Note: external fact as state-transition trigger + verifiable private decision + physical-to-digital routing (cross-pollination) → a proven purchase and private physical excursion jointly route a bounded onchain make-good.

IDEA 3: RECALL KILL SWITCH
Problem: A field operator reimbursed from an XRP treasury can still receive funds after a vehicle, device, or access credential is recalled, because the confidential signer may be unavailable and yesterday's valid spending authorization remains active.
Mechanism: A recall capsule privately resolves asset status and operator exceptions, rotates a Smart Account into a reversible safe mode, and routes pending FXRP reimbursement to hold or approved backup instead of the recalled operator.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare Smart Account authorization can make a physical recall immediately change who may receive or move FAssets without publishing operator identity, route, or incident details.
Sponsor Fit: Smart Account authorization is the necessary interoperable primitive that revokes the old capability and installs a time-bounded safe-mode route for pending FXRP; FCC/FCE privately evaluates recall record, operator credential, incident severity, reimbursement purpose, beneficiary fallback, and machine-liveness evidence, signs rotate/hold/reassign, and supplies a result the account module verifies before authorization or value changes.
Demo Hook: The judge clicks RECALL ASSET while an FXRP reimbursement is queued; the old operator's spend attempt fails, the account visibly enters SAFE MODE, and the pending amount routes to HOLD with a signed recovery receipt—even when the primary machine is marked OFFLINE.
Competitor-Derived Insight: Treasury policy systems separate key custody from authorization and continuity systems use absence evidence for recovery, but infrastructure failure and policy rotation rarely complete a user-valued asset correction in the same capsule.
Missing Outcome: Confidential, reversible financial containment for a physical recall that survives primary signer loss and handles already-pending value.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: Smart Account authorization rotation and bounded FXRP rerouting → revoke stale spending power and contain pending reimbursement value
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE recall-and-continuity resolution → reveal only rotate/hold/reassign, capability hash, amount cap, policy version, and challenge deadline
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the recall produces an alert but cannot revoke the stale authority or protect the queued FXRP
  - Remove confidential primitive: incident facts, operator identity, route, fallback beneficiary, and recovery policy must be public, or an administrator gains unilateral power to seize the reimbursement
Proof Path: judge queues an FXRP reimbursement and triggers a recall capsule → Smart Account exposes current capability and pending asset state → primary machine failure is injected and registered fallback FCC/FCE privately checks recall, liveness, and exception policy → signed safe-mode rotation is contract-verified → old authorization is revoked and FXRP is held or reassigned under a reversible challenge window → judge receives old/new capability hashes, machine rotation status, signed result, rejected old spend, asset-routing transaction, challenge timer, and abstention when fallback identity or policy version is stale
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are operator identity, asset identifier, recall detail, incident severity, route, reimbursement purpose, fallback beneficiary, and recovery thresholds; revealed output is rotate/hold/reassign, amount cap, capability hash, version, reason class, and challenge deadline. The UI labels both primary and fallback machine identities and their registered/attested/simulated states; the account verifies approved signer rotation, policy hash, nonce, bounds, and expiry. The physical recall feed and offline event are deterministic failure fixtures labeled simulated; Smart Account rotation, spend rejection, and FXRP routing are verifiable contract behavior.
Build Slice: One Smart Account recovery module and one FXRP hold vault, one FCC/FCE recall function with primary/fallback signer paths, and a UI containing queue, recall, offline, challenge, and release controls; two contracts total.
Adaptation Note: policy-controlled autonomous execution + multi-party consensus for high-stakes action + physical-to-digital routing and local safety boundary (cross-pollination) → a physical recall becomes a reversible onchain authority rotation that contains already-moving value.
