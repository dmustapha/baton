# Generator A — Underserved Users and Costly Jobs

IDEA 1: ORPHAN MINT RESCUE
Problem: An XRPL wallet user who paid into an FXRP mint flow but closed the app after a tag mismatch or proof delay can strand value and spend hours proving ownership to support.
Mechanism: A rescue escrow privately matches an abandoned XRP payment to its legitimate claimant and permits exactly one corrected FXRP mint-or-refund transition.
Chain-Native Angle: The problem exists because one user intent crosses XRPL payment evidence, FAssets mint state, and a Flare recipient; only Flare can join the external payment to the resulting FXRP consequence.
Sponsor Fit: A real FXRP lifecycle transition moves the rescued claim into minted FXRP or an explicit refund branch, while FCC/FCE privately evaluates the claimant's wallet-control proof, original intent secret, destination policy, and duplicate-claim status, signs a minimal rescue authorization, and supplies a result the rescue contract verifies before value moves.
Demo Hook: The judge watches an apparently abandoned XRP payment turn from ORPHANED to RESCUED, then sees FXRP arrive at the corrected Flare address beside the source payment, confidential authorization, and destination receipt.
Competitor-Derived Insight: The asset-entry cluster makes happy-path minting legible, while continuity and exception-resolution mechanisms show that negative evidence and bounded recovery are more valuable than another route dashboard.
Missing Outcome: Safe intervention for a payment observed after abandonment, with proof that the claimant cannot redirect or claim it twice.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: real XRP-to-FXRP transition → complete or safely terminate an interrupted mint intent
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE claimant-and-intent reconciliation → reveal only rescue/deny, destination commitment, amount cap, expiry, and reason code needed for the transition
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: no stranded external payment becomes usable FXRP or reaches a terminal refund state, so there is nothing economically rescued
  - Remove confidential primitive: wallet-control evidence and the intended destination must be disclosed publicly or an operator can redirect the claim, so safe self-service rescue fails
Proof Path: claimant submits an orphan intent ID and private ownership evidence → XRPL payment and incomplete FXRP state are observed → attested FCC/FCE reconciles payment, intent, destination, and prior claims → signed rescue permit is verified by the Flare contract → the real FXRP mint/release transition executes → judge receives linked source-payment, machine-status, permit, Flare transaction, final-balance receipts; mismatch, expiry, or duplicate evidence produces a signed abstention and no movement
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are the intent secret, wallet-control proof, intended recipient, and recovery preference; revealed output is a bounded rescue/deny permit with commitments, amount, expiry, and reason code. The UI displays the FCC/FCE machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status, and the contract verifies the authorized machine signature, nonce, intent hash, and expiry. If live FAssets rescue hooks are unavailable, the demo labels a fixture lifecycle as simulated and never represents it as a production mint.
Build Slice: One rescue-verifier contract plus one FXRP escrow adapter, a Next.js claimant flow, one FCC/FCE reconciliation function, and a scripted orphan payment fixture or live test transition; two contracts total.
Adaptation Note: external fact as state-transition trigger + pre-action and post-action guardian + closed-loop diagnosis and action (cross-pollination) → a private, single-use correction permit for an interrupted mint rather than a support ticket.

IDEA 2: QUORUM FALLBACK PAYROLL
Problem: A small team treasury with an unavailable or compromised signer can miss contractor payroll, while emergency key sharing exposes salary rules and creates unrestricted control over the whole treasury.
Mechanism: An expiring confidential fallback policy rotates a Smart Account's authorization for one payroll batch, pays only committed recipients and totals, then automatically removes the temporary authority.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare Smart Account authorization can turn a private continuity decision into narrowly scoped FXRP payroll execution without handing a recovery operator the treasury key.
Sponsor Fit: Smart Account authorization is the necessary interoperable primitive that installs and consumes a one-batch capability, while FCC/FCE privately checks the committed payroll file, signer-liveness evidence, quorum approvals, treasury bounds, and policy version, signs the batch root and temporary authorization, and lets the account contract verify it before executing FXRP transfers.
Demo Hook: The primary signer is toggled OFF; three hidden salaries still pay in one click under a temporary authorization, after which a second attempted payment visibly fails because the capability has erased itself.
Competitor-Derived Insight: The treasury cluster proves policy-gated signing removes operator custody, but its underserved state is machine or signer loss; the payments cluster supplies a clear existing payer-payee relationship with no marketplace cold start.
Missing Outcome: Recovery from signer unavailability that preserves confidential payroll terms and cannot silently become permanent treasury control.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: Smart Account authorization → grant and consume a narrowly bounded FXRP payroll capability
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE fallback-quorum and batch evaluation → reveal only batch root, aggregate cap, validity window, and rotate/deny status
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the fallback decision cannot safely change who may execute the treasury's asset transfers, so payroll remains blocked
  - Remove confidential primitive: salaries, quorum evidence, and contingency thresholds become public or the temporary signer can invent a batch, so private bounded continuity fails
Proof Path: treasury admin triggers missed-payroll recovery → Smart Account shows the old authorization and funded FXRP balance → FCC/FCE privately checks liveness, quorum, batch, and current policy → signed temporary batch authorization is verified onchain → Smart Account pays the committed FXRP leaves and revokes the capability → judge sees old/new authorization, aggregate-only signed result, transfers, zero remaining allowance, and a rejected replay; failed quorum or stale policy yields abstention
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are salary rows, recipient salts, quorum approvals, liveness evidence, and emergency thresholds; revealed output is rotate/deny, batch root, total cap, expiry, and policy version. Machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status are explicit in the receipt; the Smart Account verifier checks the FCC/FCE signature, policy hash, nonce, root, cap, and expiry. A simulated liveness probe is labeled as such, while authorization consumption and asset transfers remain contract-verifiable.
Build Slice: One minimal Smart Account module and one batch distributor/verifier contract, a three-recipient payroll UI, one confidential policy function, and tests for success, stale policy, over-cap, and replay; two contracts total.
Adaptation Note: policy-controlled autonomous execution + privacy-preserving value flow + forced memory scarcity (cross-pollination) → a payroll authority that exists for one confidential batch and is deliberately forgotten after use.

IDEA 3: LATEPAY RETURN ROUTER
Problem: A merchant receiving XRP after a checkout quote expires must manually identify the order, calculate a fair refund, locate the safe return address, and expose invoice details to an operator, making small late payments costlier to resolve than their value.
Mechanism: A confidential returns clerk converts a late-payment proof into a policy-bounded PMW-signed XRP refund or an explicit hold receipt without revealing the invoice or merchant rules.
Chain-Native Angle: Flare is the coordination and verification layer where XRPL payment evidence, confidential merchant policy, and a PMW external-chain action become one auditable refund lifecycle.
Sponsor Fit: PMW is load-bearing because its policy-held key performs the actual XRP refund on XRPL, while FCC/FCE privately evaluates the invoice, quote timestamp, fulfillment state, return-address binding, fee tolerance, and merchant refund rules, signs refund/hold plus exact bounds, and provides a decision the Flare verifier contract validates before PMW may sign the external transaction.
Demo Hook: A red LATE PAYMENT card becomes REFUNDED as the hidden invoice is evaluated, an XRP refund appears on XRPL, and the UI pairs it with a Flare-verified decision receipt; changing the return address produces a visible refusal instead.
Competitor-Derived Insight: Payment products make value movement judge-legible, and external-evidence tools make fact quality visible; the missing merchant job is closed-loop exception handling rather than another payment alert.
Missing Outcome: Economical, privacy-preserving resolution of payments that arrive after their commercial action window.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: PMW external-chain action → sign and broadcast the bounded XRP refund to the proven source owner
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE late-payment policy execution → reveal only refund/hold, amount cap, destination commitment, expiry, and reason code
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the merchant receives a recommendation but the costly refund job remains manual and custodial
  - Remove confidential primitive: invoice terms and refund rules leak or PMW lacks a verifiable reason to distinguish a valid refund from theft
Proof Path: merchant selects a late payment → external XRP payment and expired checkout state are bound to an incident → FCC/FCE privately evaluates invoice and refund policy → signed refund permit is verified by the Flare contract and consumed by PMW → PMW signs and broadcasts the constrained XRP return → judge sees incoming payment, machine/attestation state, permit hash, outbound XRPL transaction, and reconciled terminal receipt; address mismatch, fulfilled order, stale evidence, or unavailable machine yields HOLD with no signature
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are invoice terms, fulfillment evidence, margin/fee tolerance, refund policy, and customer-address binding; revealed output is refund/hold, capped amount, destination commitment, expiry, and reason code. The receipt states PMW/FCC machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status; the Flare contract verifies the FCC/FCE signature and single-use permit, and PMW enforces the same transaction digest. If external broadcast is simulated, the UI marks it simulated and uses a signed raw-transaction hash rather than claiming settlement.
Build Slice: One permit-verifier contract, one FCC/FCE policy function, a PMW transaction builder/signing adapter, and a two-case Next.js incident screen using a test XRPL payment; one contract total.
Adaptation Note: external fact as state-transition trigger + policy-controlled autonomous execution + physical-to-digital routing (cross-pollination) → a verified fact on one chain privately routes value back on the originating chain.

IDEA 4: PARTIAL REDEMPTION PATCH
Problem: An FXRP holder or FAsset support operator facing a partially fulfilled redemption must compare external payments, deadlines, requested amounts, and recovery rules by hand, while a wrong top-up can double-pay the claimant and worsen protocol exposure.
Mechanism: A confidential counterfactual engine chooses the smallest safe make-whole action from FDC-verified redemption evidence and authorizes a capped FXRP recovery-vault payout only when it improves both claimant and vault state.
Chain-Native Angle: Partial redemption is intrinsically a Flare/FAssets edge state: FDC proves what happened externally, confidential policy resolves sensitive exposure, and a Flare vault completes the missing asset outcome.
Sponsor Fit: FDC-verified asset lifecycle evidence is load-bearing because the recovery amount depends on the proven XRP fulfillment versus the requested redemption, while FCC/FCE privately evaluates claimant evidence, agent exposure, vault limits, competing claims, and counterfactual payout choices, signs pay/abstain with a cap, and supplies a result the recovery contract verifies before releasing FXRP.
Demo Hook: The judge sees a 70%-fulfilled redemption, then a hidden three-option simulation selects a 30 FXRP patch; the vault pays exactly 30 and the lifecycle flips to MADE WHOLE, while a replay and an overpay scenario both abstain.
Competitor-Derived Insight: FAssets risk products diagnose solvency and negative evidence well, while closed-loop guardians join simulation to action; the underserved support job is executing a bounded recovery without publishing the full agent book.
Missing Outcome: A safe, policy-bounded correction for partial redemption that proves before and after state and cannot compound exposure.
Multi-Track Architecture:
  - Interoperable Asset Products: FDC-verified redemption lifecycle → establish the exact external shortfall that permits a one-time FXRP recovery payout
  - Confidential Compute Apps: FCC/FCE counterfactual recovery selection → reveal only pay/abstain, capped patch amount, lifecycle commitment, expiry, and reason code
Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:
  - Remove interoperable primitive: the contract cannot distinguish a real partial redemption from a fabricated shortfall, so no make-whole payout is safe
  - Remove confidential primitive: sensitive agent exposure and competing recovery claims must be public or the vault cannot prove the chosen patch is the least-worsening authorized action
Proof Path: holder opens a partial-redemption incident → FDC establishes requested redemption and external XRP payments → FCC/FCE privately simulates abstain, patch, and delayed recovery against current bounds → signed least-worsening result is verified by the vault → capped FXRP patch transfers and incident nonce closes → judge receives raw evidence reference, verified shortfall, machine-status receipt, simulation result, payout transaction, before/after balances, and replay rejection; conflicting or stale proof forces abstention
Trust Boundary: Private inputs are claimant documentation, agent exposure, pending claims, vault reserve threshold, and recovery priority; revealed output is pay/abstain, patch cap, evidence commitment, policy version, expiry, and reason code. Machine identity and registered/attested/simulated status appear beside the result; the vault checks the FCC/FCE signature, FDC evidence commitment, nonce, amount cap, and expiry. A mocked FDC proof or agent book is explicitly labeled fixture/simulated, while the vault transfer and verification path are demonstrated onchain.
Build Slice: One recovery vault and one evidence/result verifier, a seeded partial-redemption scenario, one confidential three-branch simulation, and a receipt UI with payout, abstention, and replay tests; two contracts total.
Adaptation Note: novel DeFi state machine + pre-action and post-action guardian + closed-loop diagnosis and action (cross-pollination) → a least-worsening confidential simulation that directly completes a proven partial redemption.
