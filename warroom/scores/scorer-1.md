### Dual-Track Depth

#### Interoperable Asset Depth — 9/10

- **Primitive:** Flare Smart Account session authorization over a real bounded FXRP action.
- **Necessity:** The product promise is that stale authority becomes economically incapable of moving value, which requires the account to enforce asset, target, amount, operation, cycle, nonce, and expiry.
- **Removal-test result:** Pass; `gate-report.md`, S4 states that without Smart Account enforcement the signed cap cannot control value.
- **Proof evidence:** `raw-idea-pool.md`, MANDATE ZERO specifies an expired mandate rejection, exact 20 FXRP execution, spent-mandate state, and over-cap/replay refusal receipts; `gate-report.md` additionally requires actual Coston2 enforcement.
- **Operational risk:** Live Smart Account availability, typed account integration, and exact Coston2 enforcement must be proven before UI work; a custom vault that merely imitates expiry would reduce this score sharply.

#### Confidential Compute Depth — 8/10

- **Primitive:** FCC/FCE policy computation over private obligations, reserve target, exposure, destination rules, prior-cycle receipt, and signer health, producing a signed one-cycle cap.
- **Necessity:** Zero authority may be recreated only from current hidden constraints; a public fixed cap would remove the privacy-dependent product mechanic.
- **Removal-test result:** Pass; `gate-report.md`, S4 says authority cannot be safely recreated without FCC.
- **Proof evidence:** `raw-idea-pool.md` defines the signed result fields `{account, operation, asset, amountCap, destinationClass, cycle, expiry}` and contract verification of signer, policy version, nonce, target, and amount.
- **Operational risk:** The active brief §8 states FCC is not fully public and may require hosted or self-hosted indexer access; simulated TEE output or a local signer is disallowed by the gate.

#### Joined Product Coherence — 9/10

- **Joined mechanism:** deterministic Smart Account decay creates the blocked economic state; FCC alone can derive the next cap; the account alone can enforce it on FXRP.
- **Necessity:** Neither track is a second feature tab: confidential computation produces the sole valid authorization and the interoperable account makes that authorization consequential.
- **Removal-test result:** Pass in both directions under `gate-report.md`, S4.
- **Proof evidence:** The joined path is one continuous demonstration: expired transfer refusal → FCC result → exact FXRP action → spent mandate → replay and over-cap refusal.
- **Operational risk:** Coherence collapses if the demo uses a locally signed cap or a non-Smart-Account proxy contract, so the exact sponsor path is a hard acceptance condition.

### Scorer 1 Verdict

Mandate Zero is a coherent dual-track product with a compact, memorable failure-first demo. Its main weakness is evidence maturity: the critical FCC and Smart Account receipts are requirements, not yet demonstrated artifacts.

### Shadow Scores

| Shadow criterion | Score | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|

| Generative Competitor Leverage | 8/10 | `raw-idea-pool.md` derives directly from C6 split-brain policy, quorum change, and now-invalid signatures plus C12 refusal receipts, then closes the missing outcome with a supplier payment. |

### Dual-Track Depth

#### Interoperable Asset Depth — 9/10

- **Primitive:** Protocol Managed Wallet execution of one bounded external XRPL payment.
- **Necessity:** The confidential intersection matters only because it becomes the exact external payment without exposing unrestricted raw-key custody.
- **Removal-test result:** Pass; `gate-report.md`, S3 states that without PMW no external payment occurs.
- **Proof evidence:** `raw-idea-pool.md`, SPLITLOCK requires the PMW payment, XRPL transaction hash, recipient balance, 701 rejection, and replay refusal; `gate-report.md` disallows local PMW emulation.
- **Operational risk:** PMW access and real external signing are hard dependencies, and XRPL broadcast/reconciliation expands the failure surface beyond Coston2 contract execution.

#### Confidential Compute Depth — 8/10

- **Primitive:** FCC/FCE private intersection computation across two full policy versions, quorum evidence, supplier terms, internal limits, invoice amount, and machine state.
- **Necessity:** Public comparison leaks both policies, while PMW cannot safely choose an amount from conflicting hidden rules without the signed intersection or abstention.
- **Removal-test result:** Pass; `gate-report.md`, S3 says no safe amount can be derived without revealing policies when FCC is removed.
- **Proof evidence:** `raw-idea-pool.md` binds the result to `{intentHash, destination, amount, resolvedVersions, expiry}` or a signed abstention reason and requires Solidity verification of both commitments, nonce, and deadline.
- **Operational risk:** Correctness requires deterministic policy normalization and intersection semantics as well as real FCC machine identity/attestation; ambiguous policy language or simulated attestation invalidates the result.

#### Joined Product Coherence — 9/10

- **Joined mechanism:** a conflicting external-payment intent enters FCC, the safe intersection becomes the only PMW instruction, and the XRPL receipt proves that exact private resolution moved value.
- **Necessity:** FCC and PMW form one authorization chain; removing either leaves respectively an unsafe signer or a non-economic private verdict.
- **Removal-test result:** Pass in both directions under `gate-report.md`, S3.
- **Proof evidence:** The 900/700/701 sequence plus the no-overlap abstention is a single causal proof path with both success and failure receipts.
- **Operational risk:** Joined coherence is also the concept's execution fragility: FCC, verifier, PMW, XRPL submission, and reconciliation must all be live in sequence for the product to exist.

### Scorer 1 Verdict

SplitLock has excellent sponsor depth and a sharper confidential-computation problem than a generic treasury guardian. Its live dependency chain is too brittle to outrank the narrower survivor unless real FCC-to-PMW access is already proven.

## Final Ranking

1. **Mandate Zero**
2. **SplitLock**

**Strongest reason for the ordering:** Mandate Zero proves the joined mechanic through one expiring Smart Account cycle and one bounded Coston2 asset action, while SplitLock must successfully traverse real FCC, PMW, XRPL broadcast, and external reconciliation; the gate report explicitly classifies that second chain as high operational risk.
