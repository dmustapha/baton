# Round 6 — Generator D

## 1. Just-in-Time FXRP Line

- **Name:** Just-in-Time FXRP Line
- **Problem:** A holder must choose between keeping FXRP productive in a vault and keeping it liquid enough for an XRPL-authorized Flare application action.
- **Market Anchor:** Existing FXRP holders already deposit into applications, while Smart Account users encode XRPL instructions and pay executors for atomic Flare calls; vault returns and executor fees are existing economic signals.
- **Named Buyer:** An XRP owner who controls a Flare Personal Account containing an existing FXRP vault position.
- **Existing Workflow:** The holder manually redeems vault shares to liquid FXRP, constructs and signs an XRPL Smart Account instruction, waits for proof and execution, then manually redeposits any unused FXRP.
- **Current Substitute:** Leave FXRP idle before a planned action, or perform separate vault-withdrawal, Smart Account, application, and redeposit transactions.
- **Mechanism:** Analogous to checking-account overdraft backed by savings, the holder preauthorizes one exact application category, maximum FXRP draw, destination, deadline, and minimum output. The machine moves `Yielding -> DrawCommitted` only when the XRPL instruction commits those terms; after FDC verification it calls `IERC4626.redeem()` for the exact shortfall, then the Personal Account invokes a verified AMM `exactInput` or lending `supply` call. Successful execution moves to `Settled` and returns unused FXRP to the vault only if the signed instruction says so. A failed target call moves to `LiquidReturned`, returning withdrawn FXRP to holder control rather than retrying or choosing another route.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** An XRPL signature causes already-owned, yield-bearing FXRP on Flare to change from vault shares into exact application liquidity without giving an executor discretionary asset authority; Smart Accounts, FDC, and programmable FXRP are jointly causal.
- **Sponsor Fit:** Flare Smart Accounts carry the XRPL authorization, FDC proves it, FXRP is withdrawn through a verified `IERC4626.redeem()` interface and consumed by a named application interface, and Contract Registry resolves mutable protocol addresses. It targets Interoperable Asset Products only.
- **Demo Hook:** Keep 100 FXRP visibly earning, sign a 7-FXRP XRPL instruction, and show the Personal Account pull exactly 7 FXRP from the vault and execute a lending `supply` call; replay the same instruction and show the nonce refusal, then force target failure and show the 7 FXRP return liquid to the holder.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight:** Smart Account execution is already possible, but the missing outcome is holder-controlled composition that starts from a live position and ends in a meaningful application state, not merely freshly minted or idle FXRP.
- **Missing Outcome:** FXRP that remains productive until the precise moment an externally authorized application action needs it.
- **Multi-Track Architecture (single-track):** One Interoperable Asset Products lifecycle: FXRP vault shares -> XRPL-committed draw -> callable Flare application -> settled position or liquid holder return. No confidentiality claim.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove Smart Account/FDC authorization and the draw is no longer controlled from XRP; remove FXRP vault redemption or the concrete target call and the product becomes an ordinary relay rather than a just-in-time interoperable asset line.
- **Proof Path:** Vault deposit/share state -> XRPL transaction with nonce, cap, target, deadline, and calldata commitment -> FDC proof -> exact `redeem()` event -> Personal Account target-call event -> resulting lending balance or AMM output -> unused/failed FXRP destination; replay and target-failure cases prove bounds.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** The holder owns FXRP, controls the XRPL account and Personal Account, signs every draw's amount/target/deadline/output bounds, and receives all residue. The executor only relays the committed instruction and cannot change it. The vault executes `redeem()`, the Personal Account enforces the call, the selected application executes it, and FDC/public events let holder and judge verify. No invented operator, budget, or discretionary transfer exists.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts overdraft protection but reverses its economics: no lender extends credit and no debt arises; the “line” is the holder's own productive FXRP, made callable only by a one-use cross-chain mandate. That causal authorization transition keeps it from being a generic sweep.

## 2. FXRP Snowball

- **Name:** FXRP Snowball
- **Problem:** An FXRP holder with a live lending debt must repeatedly decide whether small vault gains are worth harvesting, swapping, and applying to debt, so gains often remain idle relative to an accumulating obligation.
- **Market Anchor:** FXRP holders already use vaults and lending markets, perform swaps, and pay borrowing costs; repeated DeFi use and fees prove the workflow without requiring a new counterparty.
- **Named Buyer:** A self-custodied FXRP holder who has both a yield-bearing FXRP vault position and a debt in a verified Flare lending market.
- **Existing Workflow:** The holder reads vault value and debt separately, manually redeems profitable shares, swaps FXRP to the debt asset, calls the lending market's `repay(asset, amount, rateMode, onBehalfOf)`-category interface, and later decides what to do after debt reaches zero.
- **Current Substitute:** Manual repayments from wallet funds, leaving gains compounding while debt accrues, or fully unwinding the FXRP position to repay at once.
- **Mechanism:** Borrowing from the consumer “debt snowball,” the holder sets an immutable principal floor, minimum gain lot, one debt market, and maximum slippage. The machine moves `EarningWithDebt -> GainReady -> PayingDown`: only vault value above the floor may be redeemed; the verified AMM swaps that exact gain lot into the debt asset and `repay()` applies it directly to the holder's debt. When the lending market reports zero debt, the contract irreversibly changes to `DebtCleared`; all future gain calls return FXRP to the holder and can never swap or repay without a fresh mandate. If swap or repay cannot complete atomically, the gain remains FXRP under holder control.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** Productive FXRP crosses two Flare application states—vault gain and lending liability—and the debt-zero event removes a previously valid asset path. Programmable FXRP is not collateral decoration; its realized productivity causes the economic state change.
- **Sponsor Fit:** FAssets-derived FXRP, a verified ERC-4626-style `convertToAssets()/redeem()` vault, verified AMM `exactInput`, lending `debtBalance/repay` interfaces, and Contract Registry resolution provide the full application product. It targets Interoperable Asset Products only.
- **Demo Hook:** Begin with 100 FXRP principal, 4 FXRP realized gain, and a visible 3-unit debt; one call redeems only gain, swaps exactly enough, repays debt to zero, and flips the screen to `DebtCleared`; a second call proves no further FXRP can be swapped under the expired mandate.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight:** Participant economics and before/after ownership are persuasive only when diagnosis closes into an exact application call with measurable avoided borrowing cost.
- **Missing Outcome:** A holder-funded debt payoff mode that automatically ceases to exist at zero debt instead of becoming an open-ended asset manager.
- **Multi-Track Architecture (single-track):** One Interoperable Asset Products path: FXRP vault surplus -> application swap -> existing lending debt reduction -> authority expiry at debt zero.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove FXRP vault surplus and there is no holder-owned snowball fuel; remove the callable lending `repay` and debt-zero read, and the defining transition and self-termination disappear.
- **Proof Path:** Principal/share checkpoint -> vault asset-value read -> surplus-only `redeem()` -> AMM input/output events -> lending `repay` and debt before/after -> `DebtCleared` transition -> rejected post-clear swap; atomic failure demonstrates that principal and unmatched gain cannot leak.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** The holder funds FXRP, approves the vault and capped snowball contract, selects their own debt account and fixed market, sets principal/slippage bounds, and receives residual value. A permissionless caller may advance only a currently valid gain lot. Vault, AMM, and lending contracts expose the exact reads/calls; nobody may create debt, change beneficiary, spend principal, or revive the mandate after zero without the holder's signature.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts the household debt-snowball rule to asset productivity rather than salary. Its novel causal transition is authority destruction at verified zero debt; it is not generic portfolio automation, a liquidation guard, or third-party repayment subsidy.

## 3. Yield Layaway

- **Name:** Yield Layaway
- **Problem:** An FXRP holder may want a position in a second application but not want to carve up existing principal or fund the new position from outside assets.
- **Market Anchor:** Current holders already keep FXRP in vaults and move FXRP among applications; application minimums, vault returns, and repeated deposits provide an existing flow and economic reason to act.
- **Named Buyer:** A self-custodied FXRP holder with a productive vault position who wants to open one specified FXRP lending or vault position using only realized gain.
- **Existing Workflow:** The holder watches the source vault, manually estimates gain, redeems some shares, waits until enough FXRP is available for a worthwhile second deposit, and calls the destination application's deposit or `supply` interface.
- **Current Substitute:** Reduce principal immediately, fund the destination from wallet FXRP, or leave the desired second position unopened.
- **Mechanism:** Analogous to retail layaway, the holder names one destination application, target FXRP amount, source principal floor, deadline, and destination calldata before charging begins. The machine moves `Earning -> LayawayFunding` as `IERC4626.redeem()` releases only verified surplus into a non-transferable internal balance. At the target it becomes `PurchaseReady`; only the holder may call `Complete`, which atomically invokes the destination's verified `deposit()` or lending `supply()` and delivers the resulting shares/position to the holder. If the deadline passes, state becomes `Expired` and all accumulated FXRP is immediately withdrawable by the holder; it cannot be redirected, extended, or completed without a new signature.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** Yield produced by one FXRP application matures into the right to open a different FXRP application position while source principal remains intact. The asset's application-to-application lifecycle, rather than a payment or wrapper token, is the product.
- **Sponsor Fit:** FXRP is the sole funding asset, the source uses a verified ERC-4626-style `convertToAssets()/redeem()` interface, the destination is a concrete verified vault `deposit()` or lending `supply()` interface, and Contract Registry resolves any protocol addresses. It targets Interoperable Asset Products only.
- **Demo Hook:** Show a 5-FXRP target charge from three visible source-vault gain events, reveal `PurchaseReady`, then press Complete to receive destination vault shares without reducing the 100-FXRP source floor; run an expired target and show only holder withdrawal remains callable.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight:** “FXRP became immediately useful” is strongest when a live position produces another user-owned application outcome, while forced expiry prevents stale authorization from masquerading as convenience.
- **Missing Outcome:** A no-new-principal route from existing FXRP productivity into a precommitted second DeFi position.
- **Multi-Track Architecture (single-track):** One Interoperable Asset Products composition: source FXRP application gain -> bounded layaway state -> holder-owned destination application position or expiry return.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove the source application's realized FXRP gain and layaway has no endogenous funding; remove the precommitted destination interface and target-share delivery and it degenerates into a savings balance rather than an interoperable product.
- **Proof Path:** Source principal/share checkpoint -> successive surplus-only redemption events -> exact internal FXRP balance -> deadline/target transition -> holder completion signature -> destination `deposit/supply` event -> destination shares or balance owned by holder; expired completion rejection and withdrawal prove the safeguard.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** The holder owns the source shares, approves a capped surplus path, preselects target/deadline/calldata, alone completes or withdraws, and directly receives the destination position. Permissionless callers may add only currently verified surplus to the fixed target. Source and destination applications execute their published interfaces; no seller, sponsor, marketplace, or project-funded transfer is assumed.
- **Adaptation Note:** Retail layaway normally reserves a merchant's good with installments; here no merchant or escrow claim exists. The remixed primitive makes one application's realized yield cross a threshold that unlocks a second, predeclared application state, with expiry destroying the target authority.

## 4. One-Exit Reserve

- **Name:** One-Exit Reserve
- **Problem:** An FXRP holder who deploys nearly everything can earn more, but may later discover that the liquid remainder is too small or fragmented for the native XRP exit they actually want.
- **Market Anchor:** FXRP holders already divide assets between DeFi and liquid balances and use native redemption; opportunity cost, mandatory redemption fees, and protocol minimums create a real reserve-versus-yield tradeoff.
- **Named Buyer:** A self-custodied FXRP holder who wants most FXRP productive while continuously preserving one exact holder-chosen XRP exit amount.
- **Existing Workflow:** The holder manually leaves some FXRP idle, checks current Asset Manager redemption parameters, deposits the rest through `IERC4626.deposit()`, and later withdraws enough additional shares before requesting redemption.
- **Current Substitute:** Keep a guessed FXRP buffer idle, deploy everything and hope withdrawal remains available, or manually rebalance after every deposit, withdrawal, and redemption.
- **Mechanism:** Adapting the consumer emergency-fund rule, the holder chooses one target redemption amount and XRP recipient. The machine has a strict priority cycle: `ReserveBuilding` accepts holder FXRP and vault withdrawals until the liquid balance equals that exact target; only then can state become `ExcessProductive`, allowing additional FXRP into the verified vault. The reserve is never spendable by application automation. Only the holder can move `ReserveReady -> Redeeming` through the current Asset Manager redemption call. Once XRP payment is proven, state returns to `ReserveBuilding`, disabling new vault deposits until the holder deliberately rebuilds the next exit. Current protocol minimums can block readiness but can never silently resize the holder's target.
- **Chain-Native Angle:** The reserve is defined by a valid FXRP-to-XRP lifecycle, not a fiat-value dashboard threshold. A proven external XRP payment consumes the reserved state and causally closes the vault-deposit gate for the next cycle.
- **Sponsor Fit:** FAssets/FXRP and the published Asset Manager redemption interface define the reserve and discharge; FDC proves XRP payment; a verified ERC-4626 `deposit()/redeem()` application makes only the excess productive; Contract Registry resolves current protocol addresses. It targets Interoperable Asset Products only.
- **Demo Hook:** Deposit 120 FXRP against a 20-FXRP exit target and show exactly 20 remain non-callable reserve while 100 enter the vault; redeem the 20 to XRP, prove payment with FDC, then show a new excess deposit rejected until the holder rebuilds the reserve.
- **Competitor-Derived Insight:** Redemption readiness is an unserved economic outcome, but a useful product must change what assets are allowed to do and complete a native action rather than display an exit estimate.
- **Missing Outcome:** A holder-readable rule—“one exact XRP exit before extra yield”—whose successful cross-chain discharge changes future application eligibility.
- **Multi-Track Architecture (single-track):** One Interoperable Asset Products cycle: exact liquid FXRP reserve -> excess vault deployment -> holder-triggered native redemption -> proven XRP payment -> reserve-priority reset.
- **Per-Track Load-Bearing Test:** Remove FAssets redemption/FDC and reserve consumption has no cross-chain completion signal; remove the callable FXRP vault and the reserve priority no longer governs a useful competing economic action.
- **Proof Path:** Holder target/recipient signature -> FXRP reserve and excess balances -> exact vault `deposit()` event -> rejected reserve-spend call -> Asset Manager redemption request -> FDC-proven XRP payment -> state reset -> rejected excess deposit while under target; before/after ownership remains public.
- **Authority and Integration Map:** The holder supplies FXRP, fixes and changes the target only before a cycle, alone requests XRP redemption, controls the XRP recipient, and receives vault shares and XRP. The contract can deposit only balance above target into the selected vault and cannot lend, transfer, or swap the reserve. Asset Manager performs native redemption, the assigned protocol agent pays under protocol rules, and FDC verifies; no invented custodian or emergency manager exists.
- **Adaptation Note:** Adapts an emergency fund without insurance, loss prediction, or a guardian. Its causal novelty is that verified consumption of one native redemption lot disables future productive deployment until the holder rebuilds it, making the FAssets lifecycle an enforceable economic priority rather than a balance alert.
